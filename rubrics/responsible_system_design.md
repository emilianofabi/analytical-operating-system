# Rubric: Responsible System Design

## Purpose

This rubric evaluates whether a student can design, evaluate, and govern technical or AI systems as sociotechnical systems with stakeholders, harms, uncertainty, accountability, monitoring, and lifecycle responsibility.

## Scale

1. Emerging
2. Developing
3. Proficient
4. Advanced

## Criteria

| Criterion | 1 Emerging | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| System Boundary | Treats the model or tool as the whole system. | Identifies some surrounding context. | Defines technical, human, organizational, and institutional boundaries. | Includes feedback loops, downstream effects, governance structures, and lifecycle dynamics. |
| Stakeholder Identification | Stakeholders are generic or incomplete. | Direct users are identified. | Direct and indirect stakeholders are mapped. | Stakeholder mapping includes affected non-users, vulnerable groups, decision-makers, and accountability holders. |
| Harm and Risk Analysis | Harms are vague or absent. | Obvious harms are listed. | Harms are categorized by likelihood, severity, and affected group. | Harm analysis includes cumulative effects, power asymmetries, contestability, and second-order consequences. |
| Data and Measurement Analysis | Data is treated as neutral input. | Data source is described. | Data provenance, labels, measurement bias, missingness, and representation are analyzed. | Data-generating process is connected to institutional history, incentives, and deployment risk. |
| Technical Evaluation | Uses only aggregate performance. | Includes limited subgroup or robustness checks. | Evaluates performance, error types, calibration, robustness, and subgroup variation. | Evaluation is tied to deployment thresholds, monitoring plans, and decision-specific risk tolerance. |
| Governance Design | Governance is absent or generic. | Mentions oversight or documentation. | Specifies documentation, review, monitoring, human oversight, appeal, and rollback. | Governance includes lifecycle review, incident response, accountability assignment, auditability, and revision triggers. |
| Deployment Judgment | Assumes deployment if the system works technically. | Recommends deployment with weak conditions. | Provides a clear deploy, revise, delay, or reject recommendation. | Recommendation includes evidence thresholds, unresolved risks, monitoring requirements, and institutional safeguards. |

## Evidence Requirements

Assessors should look for:

- system boundary diagram;
- stakeholder map;
- harm analysis;
- data documentation;
- model or system evaluation;
- fairness or robustness audit;
- governance plan;
- risk register;
- deployment recommendation;
- oral defense.

## Calibration Protocol

Artifacts should be reviewed by at least one technical reviewer and one ethical or institutional reviewer. Final scores should document how technical performance and sociotechnical risk were weighed.
