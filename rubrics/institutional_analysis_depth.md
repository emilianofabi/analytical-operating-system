# Rubric: Institutional Analysis Depth

## Purpose

This rubric evaluates whether a student can analyze markets, policies, organizations, platforms, labor systems, and governance structures as institutional arrangements shaped by incentives, constraints, power, implementation capacity, legitimacy, and distribution.

## Scale

1. Emerging
2. Developing
3. Proficient
4. Advanced

## Criteria

| Criterion | 1 Emerging | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Problem Diagnosis | Describes a surface issue without mechanism. | Identifies a problem but mechanism is incomplete. | Defines the problem and explains plausible causal or institutional mechanisms. | Compares competing diagnoses and explains why one is most credible. |
| Actor and Incentive Mapping | Actors are missing or treated generically. | Main actors are named. | Actors, incentives, constraints, and choices are mapped clearly. | Strategic adaptation, bargaining power, and institutional asymmetries are analyzed. |
| Institutional Context | Context is vague or absent. | Some relevant institutions are mentioned. | Formal and informal institutions are connected to outcomes. | Institutional design, history, authority, enforcement, and legitimacy are integrated. |
| Evidence Use | Claims are unsupported or anecdotal. | Evidence is cited but weakly interpreted. | Evidence is relevant and connected to the argument. | Evidence quality, uncertainty, external validity, and alternative explanations are assessed. |
| Implementation Analysis | Assumes recommendations can be implemented. | Notes implementation constraints. | Analyzes authority, capacity, compliance, enforcement, and administrative burden. | Designs implementation, monitoring, feedback, and revision mechanisms. |
| Distributional Analysis | Distributional effects are ignored. | Winners and losers are named. | Benefits, costs, risks, and burdens are analyzed across groups. | Distribution is connected to legitimacy, feasibility, power, and long-term institutional effects. |
| Recommendation Quality | Recommendation is vague or ideological. | Recommendation follows partially from analysis. | Recommendation is feasible and tied to diagnosis and evidence. | Recommendation includes tradeoffs, uncertainty, implementation conditions, and revision triggers. |

## Evidence Requirements

Assessors should look for:

- problem diagnosis;
- actor map;
- incentive analysis;
- institutional context;
- evidence appendix;
- implementation plan;
- distributional analysis;
- recommendation memo;
- oral defense.

## Calibration Protocol

Reviewers from at least two disciplinary perspectives should score the artifact independently. Any disagreement greater than one level on problem diagnosis, evidence use, or implementation analysis requires written reconciliation.
