# Rubric: Synthesis and Portfolio Quality

## Purpose

This rubric evaluates whether a student can produce a coherent intellectual artifact that integrates domains, makes a defensible claim, uses appropriate evidence, communicates clearly, and contributes to a professional or academic portfolio.

## Scale

1. Emerging
2. Developing
3. Proficient
4. Advanced

## Criteria

| Criterion | 1 Emerging | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Central Question | Topic is broad or unfocused. | Question is present but imprecise. | Question is clear, answerable, and significant. | Question is sharply framed and situated within a broader intellectual or professional conversation. |
| Claim and Argument | Claim is missing or descriptive only. | Claim is present but weakly supported. | Claim is explicit and supported by method and evidence. | Claim is nuanced, defensible, and anticipates objections. |
| Domain Integration | Domains are listed rather than integrated. | Some connections across domains are made. | Each domain has a clear role in the project. | Integration is necessary to the argument and produces insight unavailable from one domain alone. |
| Method and Evidence Fit | Method does not match the question. | Method partially matches the question. | Method and evidence fit the claim. | Method, evidence, assumptions, and limitations are mutually coherent and explicitly defended. |
| Artifact Design | Artifact format is poorly matched to audience. | Artifact is usable but uneven. | Artifact format fits audience, claim, and evidence. | Artifact is polished, strategically designed, and transferable across portfolio contexts. |
| Communication Clarity | Writing or presentation is confusing. | Main ideas are understandable but structure is weak. | Communication is clear, structured, and appropriately technical. | Communication is elegant, audience-aware, and intellectually distinctive. |
| Limitation and Critique | Limitations are absent or generic. | Limitations are acknowledged. | Limitations are specific and tied to conclusions. | Limitations strengthen credibility and define future work. |
| Portfolio Signal | Artifact does not clearly demonstrate competence. | Artifact demonstrates isolated skill. | Artifact demonstrates a coherent competency. | Artifact demonstrates integrated judgment and strengthens professional or academic identity. |

## Evidence Requirements

Assessors should look for:

- final artifact;
- project brief;
- claim-method-evidence table;
- technical appendix if applicable;
- limitation section;
- portfolio case study;
- public-facing summary;
- oral defense.

## Calibration Protocol

Reviewers should identify the artifact's intended audience before scoring. Portfolio quality should be judged by the artifact's ability to make competence visible, not merely by length or technical complexity.
