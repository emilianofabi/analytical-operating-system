# Rubric: Causal Reasoning Quality

## Purpose

This rubric evaluates whether a student can define, justify, and defend causal claims through explicit counterfactual reasoning and credible identification.

## Scale

1. Emerging
2. Developing
3. Proficient
4. Advanced

## Criteria

| Criterion | 1 Emerging | 2 Developing | 3 Proficient | 4 Advanced |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Causal Question | Question is vague or associational. | Treatment and outcome are named. | Treatment, outcome, population, and estimand are clear. | Estimand is distinguished from estimator, sample, and decision target. |
| Counterfactual Logic | Counterfactual is absent. | Counterfactual is implied but weak. | Counterfactual comparison is explicit. | Alternative counterfactuals are considered and defended. |
| Identification Strategy | Method is chosen mechanically. | Design partially fits the question. | Design fits the causal structure and available data. | Design is defended against alternative methods and threats. |
| Assumption Defense | Assumptions are unstated or generic. | Assumptions are listed. | Assumptions are tied to identification. | Assumptions are evaluated for plausibility, fragility, and diagnostic evidence. |
| Threats to Validity | Threats are missing. | Generic threats are listed. | Design-specific threats are analyzed. | Threats are prioritized and matched to robustness or sensitivity checks. |
| Interpretation Discipline | Claims exceed evidence. | Causal language is inconsistent. | Conclusions are proportional to design. | Interpretation separates effect, mechanism, external validity, and decision relevance. |

## Evidence Requirements

Assessors should look for:

- treatment definition;
- outcome definition;
- population definition;
- estimand;
- DAG or causal diagram;
- identification memo;
- robustness checks;
- limitation statement;
- oral defense.

## Calibration Protocol

Reviewers should independently score the identification strategy before reviewing numerical results. Estimation quality cannot compensate for unidentified causal claims.
