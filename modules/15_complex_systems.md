# Module 15: Complex Systems, Networks, Simulation, and Emergent Behavior

## Track
Methods & Systems

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate systems judgment: the ability to analyze how macro-level patterns emerge from local interactions among agents, networks, feedback loops, constraints, adaptation, and nonlinear dynamics.

## Governing Principles

1. Systems are made of relationships, not isolated parts.
2. Local rules can generate global patterns.
3. Feedback loops drive dynamics.
4. Nonlinearity breaks simple proportional intuition.
5. Network structure shapes diffusion, fragility, and resilience.
6. Adaptive agents change the system they inhabit.
7. Efficiency and resilience are different objectives.
8. Simulation is an argument about mechanisms, not proof by animation.

## Core Questions

- What are the components of the system?
- How are they connected?
- What flows through the system?
- What feedback loops amplify or stabilize behavior?
- What network structure matters?
- What local rules generate the observed macro-pattern?
- What parameters create tipping points or regime shifts?
- How might intervention change agent behavior?
- What assumptions drive the simulation?

## Lesson 1

### Title
Local Rules, Global Patterns

### Central Problem
Complex systems analysis begins from the recognition that collective outcomes often emerge from interactions among parts. The observed pattern may not be directly intended by any individual component, but may arise from local rules, feedback, constraints, and network structure.
