# Module 16: Macroeconomics, Political Economy, and Institutional Systems

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate structural economic judgment: the ability to analyze economies as institutional, monetary, financial, technological, political, and distributive systems.

## Governing Principles

1. Economies are institutional systems.
2. Money is not a neutral veil.
3. Aggregate demand matters.
4. Productive capacity and productivity matter.
5. Expectations shape present behavior.
6. Distribution is macroeconomic.
7. Technology reorganizes production, labor, firms, and power.
8. Economic policy is made inside political conflict and institutional constraint.

## Lesson 1

### Title
The Economy as an Institutional System

### Central Problem
Macroeconomics begins with aggregate variables, but those variables are produced through institutions: households, firms, banks, governments, laws, technologies, expectations, and distributional conflicts. The economy is not a natural machine; it is an organized system of production, exchange, credit, labor, and governance.

### Required Concepts

- aggregate economy
- institution
- GDP
- money
- credit
- production
- labor market
- expectations
- distribution
- policy

### Required Outputs

- short memo distinguishing GDP from the economy itself
- institutional map of an economic system
- oral-defense answer explaining why macroeconomic variables require institutional interpretation
