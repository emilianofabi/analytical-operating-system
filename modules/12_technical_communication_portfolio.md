# Module 12: Technical Communication, Portfolio Strategy, and Career Positioning

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
3

## Core Purpose

Cultivate communication judgment: the ability to make technical, analytical, and interdisciplinary work legible to different audiences through writing, visualization, documentation, presentations, portfolios, and oral defense.

## Governing Principles

1. Communication is part of technical competence.
2. A portfolio is a body of evidence.
3. Every artifact needs an audience.
4. Clarity requires structure.
5. Claims require evidence.
6. Limitations increase credibility.
7. Technical depth must be translated, not diluted.
8. Professional identity is built through coherent artifacts.

## Core Questions

- Who is the audience?
- What claim must they understand?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- What level of technical detail is appropriate?
- What should be visualized?
- What should be documented?
- What limitations should be stated?
- What does this artifact prove about competence?
- How does this fit into a larger portfolio?

## Lesson 1

### Title
A Portfolio Is a Body of Evidence

### Central Problem
A portfolio should not be a random collection of projects. It should be a structured body of evidence showing what the student can see, build, evaluate, argue, and communicate.

### Required Concepts

- claim
- evidence
- artifact
- case study
- documentation

- portfolio signal
