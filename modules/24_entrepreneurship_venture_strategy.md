# Module 24: Entrepreneurship, Startups, Venture Strategy, and Business Model Design

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
3

## Core Purpose

Cultivate venture judgment: the ability to identify valuable problems, test assumptions, design business models, analyze unit economics, evaluate markets, build minimum viable products, understand customers, and decide whether an idea should become a venture.

## Governing Principles

1. A startup is a hypothesis machine.
2. Problems matter more than ideas.
3. Customers buy outcomes, not features.
4. Distribution is often the hardest part.
5. Business models are systems of value capture.
6. Unit economics are reality checks.
7. Product-market fit is behavioral evidence.
8. Defensibility must be earned.

## Core Questions

- What problem is worth solving?
- Who has the problem?
- How painful, frequent, expensive, or urgent is it?
- Why now?
- What must be true for this venture to work?
- How will the company create value?
- How will it capture value?
- How will customers be acquired?
- Why will customers switch?
- What are the unit economics?
- What creates defensibility?
- Should this be venture-backed, bootstrapped, nonprofit, public infrastructure, or not built?

## Lesson 1

### Title
A Startup Is a Hypothesis Machine

### Central Problem
A startup begins as a bundle of unproven assumptions about customers, problems, adoption, willingness to pay, distribution, product feasibility, unit economics, competition, and timing. Entrepreneurship is the disciplined process of testing those assumptions before resources are exhausted.

### Required Concepts

- hypothesis
- customer
- problem
- willingness to pay
- adoption
- distribution
- business model
- unit economics
- MVP
- defensibility

### Required Outputs

- startup assumption map
- riskiest-assumption test plan
- oral-defense answer explaining why an interesting idea is not yet a venture
