# Module 11: Research Methods, Evidence Synthesis, and Intellectual Production

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate research judgment: the ability to turn broad interests into answerable questions, synthesize literature, design credible methods, evaluate evidence, and produce serious intellectual artifacts.

## Governing Principles

1. Research begins with a question, not a topic.
2. Literature defines the conversation.
3. Method must fit the claim.
4. Evidence must be evaluated, not merely collected.
5. Limitations are part of credibility.
6. Replication strengthens knowledge.
7. Writing is thinking.
8. Research artifacts should make assumptions visible.

## Core Questions

- What is the research question?
- What conversation does it enter?
- What claim will be made?
- What evidence is required?
- What method fits the claim?
- What assumptions are necessary?
- What would count as disconfirming evidence?
- What are the limitations?
- Who is the audience?

## Lesson 1

### Title
A Research Question Is Not a Topic

### Central Problem
Research begins when a broad area of interest is transformed into an answerable question. A topic names a domain; a research question defines an intellectual problem, a claim space, and an evidence requirement.

### Required Concepts

- topic
- research question
- claim
- literature
- method
- evidence
- scope
- contribution
- limitation

### Required Outputs

- topic-to-question transformation memo
- claim-evidence-method table
- oral-defense answer explaining why "AI and education" is not yet a research question
