# Module 20: Experimental Design, Product Analytics, and Metrics Systems

## Track
Methods & Systems

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate experimental and measurement judgment: the ability to design metrics, experiments, and analytical systems that allow organizations to learn what actually works without mistaking metric movement for value.

## Governing Principles

1. Metrics are proxies, not values.
2. Experiments are causal designs.
3. The unit of randomization matters.
4. Statistical significance is not decision significance.
5. Measurement changes behavior.
6. Guardrail metrics are required.
7. Product behavior is dynamic.
8. A result should inform a decision.

## Core Questions

- What does success mean?
- What behavior should be measured?
- What metric represents the intended value?
- What could be gamed?
- What is the treatment?
- What is the control?
- What is the randomization unit?
- What outcome matters?
- What sample size is required?
- What uncertainty remains?
- What decision follows from the result?

## Lesson 1

### Title
Metrics Are Proxies, Not Values

### Central Problem
A metric is an operational representation of value, not the value itself. If the metric is poorly defined or aggressively optimized, it can distort behavior and weaken the connection between measurement and purpose.

### Required Concepts

- metric
- proxy
- numerator
- denominator
- time window
- population
- guardrail metric
- metric gaming
- Goodhart's law
- measurement validity

### Required Outputs

- metric definition sheet
- metric tree connecting outcome and input metrics
- oral-defense answer explaining why engagement is not necessarily value
