# Module 25: Capstone Integration, Portfolio Synthesis, and Intellectual Direction

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
6

## Core Purpose

Cultivate synthesis: the ability to integrate theory, data, computation, systems design, causal reasoning, ethical analysis, economic judgment, and communication into serious intellectual artifacts.

## Governing Principles

1. Integration is not accumulation.
2. A project is an argument.
3. Capstones demonstrate judgment, not task completion.
4. Portfolios are bodies of evidence.
5. Serious work requires a skeptical reader.
6. Technical work, writing, and communication are inseparable.
7. Limitations increase credibility.
8. Intellectual identity emerges through artifacts.

## Core Questions

- What problem is worth building a capstone around?
- What claim will the project make?
- What audience is being addressed?
- What methods fit the question?
- What evidence is required?
- What assumptions must be defended?
- What artifact should result?
- What would a skeptical expert criticize?
- What does the project prove about the student's judgment?
- How does the artifact fit into a larger portfolio?

## Lesson 1

### Title
A Capstone Is an Argument

### Central Problem
A serious capstone is not a collection of tasks. It is an argument made through theory, evidence, computation, design, and communication. The first act of capstone work is to define what the project is trying to show and why that claim matters.

### Required Concepts

- research question
- claim
- audience
- method
- evidence
- artifact
- assumption
- limitation
- skeptical reader
- portfolio signal

### Required Outputs

- one-page capstone concept memo
- claim-evidence-method table
- oral-defense answer explaining what the project proves about the student's judgment
