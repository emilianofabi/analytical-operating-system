# Module 21: Industrial Organization, Platform Strategy, and Market Design

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate strategic market judgment: the ability to analyze how firms, platforms, markets, and regulators interact when competition is strategic, information is imperfect, and market structure shapes value capture.

## Governing Principles

1. Competition is strategic.
2. Market power is the ability to shape terms.
3. Price is strategy.
4. Platforms govern markets.
5. Network effects create feedback.
6. Market design shapes outcomes.
7. Regulation shapes competition.
8. AI may restructure market power.

## Core Questions

- Why do some markets become concentrated?
- What gives a firm market power?
- How do firms set prices when they are not price takers?
- When do network effects create winner-take-most dynamics?
- Why do platforms subsidize one side of a market?
- What makes a marketplace efficient or fragile?
- How do auctions allocate scarce goods?
- When does vertical integration improve coordination or foreclose competition?
- How should regulators evaluate digital market power?

## Lesson 1

### Title
Market Power and the Ability to Shape Terms

### Central Problem
Market power is not merely firm size. It is the ability to shape prices, quality, access, data flows, rules, or terms of exchange because customers, suppliers, workers, or complementors lack equally attractive alternatives.

### Required Concepts

- market power
- substitute
- barrier to entry
- elasticity
- switching cost
- network effect
- platform governance
- markup
- competition
- regulation

### Required Outputs

- market definition memo

- oral-defense answer explaining why zero monetary price does not imply absence of market power
