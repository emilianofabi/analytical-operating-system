# Module 22: Labor Economics, Education, Human Capital, and Skill Formation

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate labor and education judgment: the ability to analyze how people develop skills, enter labor markets, earn wages, adapt to technology, and move through institutions that distribute opportunity.

## Governing Principles

1. Labor is not a standard commodity.
2. Skills are multidimensional.
3. Education has multiple functions.
4. Wages are not pure productivity.
5. Search and matching create frictions.
6. Inequality is cumulative.
7. Technology changes tasks, not just jobs.
8. Institutions shape mobility.

## Core Questions

- Why do some workers earn more than others?
- What is a skill, and how is it measured?
- How do education, credentials, networks, and experience affect opportunity?
- Are wages determined by productivity, bargaining power, institutions, discrimination, or scarcity?
- When does education increase productivity, and when does it signal ability?
- Why does unemployment persist even when jobs exist?
- How does AI alter tasks, occupations, and wages?
- What policies improve mobility, learning, and labor-market outcomes?

## Lesson 1

### Title
Labor Is Not a Standard Commodity

### Central Problem
Labor differs from ordinary goods because it is inseparable from the person who supplies it. Work involves skill, time, effort, identity, dignity, bargaining power, family constraint, geography, discrimination, and institutional access.

### Required Concepts

- labor supply
- labor demand
- worker
- firm
- wage
- bargaining power
- skill
- human capital
- institution
- dignity

### Required Outputs

- labor-market institution map
- short memo explaining why wages are not pure productivity
- oral-defense answer explaining why labor cannot be analyzed exactly like a commodity
