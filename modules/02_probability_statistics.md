# Module 02: Probability, Statistics, and Quantitative Reasoning

## Track
Foundational

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate statistical judgment: the ability to reason from data under uncertainty, distinguish signal from noise, interpret estimates, quantify uncertainty, and avoid overclaiming.

## Governing Principles

1. Data is generated by processes.
2. Uncertainty must be represented, not ignored.
3. Probability is a language for uncertain reasoning.
4. Estimation requires distinguishing estimand, estimator, and estimate.
5. Statistical significance is not practical or causal significance.
6. Models require assumptions.
7. Inference must be proportional to design and evidence.
8. Visualization and diagnostics are part of statistical reasoning.

## Core Questions

- What population is being studied?
- What sample is observed?
- What variable is being measured?
- What is uncertain?
- What is the estimand?
- What assumptions support the estimate?
- What does the interval or posterior mean?
- What would make this conclusion misleading?
- What uncertainty remains?

## Lesson 1

### Title
Uncertainty, Probability, and the Discipline of Not Overclaiming

### Central Problem
Statistical reasoning begins with uncertainty. The analyst observes partial, noisy, selected information and must decide what can be inferred about a broader process. Probability provides a formal language for this uncertainty, but interpretation requires discipline.

### Required Concepts
