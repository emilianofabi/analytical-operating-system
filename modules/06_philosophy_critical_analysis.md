# Module 06: Philosophy of Science, AI, Economics, and Critical Analysis

## Track
Foundational

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate conceptual judgment: the ability to analyze assumptions, reconstruct arguments, distinguish kinds of claims, evaluate evidence, and clarify the philosophical foundations of science, economics, statistics, artificial intelligence, and ethics.

## Governing Principles

1. Concepts structure inquiry.
2. Claims differ by type: descriptive, causal, predictive, normative, mathematical, strategic, and institutional.
3. Models require epistemic interpretation.
4. Evidence does not interpret itself.
5. Explanation is not the same as prediction.
6. Formal systems encode assumptions.
7. Ethical claims require explicit values.
8. Critique should clarify, not merely dismiss.

## Core Questions

- What kind of claim is being made?
- What assumptions support it?
- What would make it true or false?
- What counts as evidence?
- What is being explained?
- What concept is doing the work?
- What is omitted by the framework?
- What values are embedded?
- What would a strong objection be?

## Lesson 1

### Title
What Kind of Claim Is This?

### Central Problem
Analytical confusion often begins when different kinds of claims are treated as interchangeable. A descriptive claim, causal claim, predictive claim, normative claim, mathematical claim, and institutional claim require different evidence, assumptions, and standards of evaluation.

### Required Concepts

- claim type
- assumption
- evidence
- explanation
- prediction
- causation
- normativity
- conceptual clarity
- argument reconstruction

### Required Outputs

- claim classification table
- short argument reconstruction
- oral-defense answer explaining why prediction is not explanation
