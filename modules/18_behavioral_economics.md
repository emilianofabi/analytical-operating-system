# Module 18: Behavioral Economics, Cognitive Bias, and Human Decision-Making

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
3

## Core Purpose

Cultivate behavioral judgment: the ability to analyze real human decision-making under bounded attention, limited information, emotion, social influence, habits, institutional pressure, and cognitive constraint.

## Governing Principles

1. Humans are boundedly rational.
2. The decision environment shapes the decision.
3. Preferences are often constructed in context.
4. Losses often loom larger than gains.
5. Attention is scarce.
6. Beliefs are social and motivated.
7. Habits and frictions matter.
8. Behavioral design has ethical stakes.

## Core Questions

- Why do people make choices that appear irrational?
- What information was actually available?
- What was salient?
- What default or friction shaped behavior?
- What social norm or identity was activated?
- What reference point mattered?
- What intervention matches the behavioral mechanism?
- When does behavioral design become manipulation?
- How should AI systems account for human cognitive limitations?

## Lesson 1

### Title
Bounded Rationality and the Decision Environment

### Central Problem
Behavioral economics begins by rejecting the assumption that humans are perfectly calculating agents. Real decisions are made under bounded attention, limited information, cognitive load, emotion, habit, and social context. The decision environment is therefore part of the explanation.

### Required Concepts

- bounded rationality
- heuristic
- bias
- decision environment
- salience
- default
- friction
- attention
- habit
- choice architecture

### Required Outputs

- decision-environment map
- short memo explaining a behavioral mechanism without merely naming a bias
- oral-defense answer explaining why bounded rationality does not mean stupidity
