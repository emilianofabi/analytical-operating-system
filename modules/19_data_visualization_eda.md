# Module 19: Data Visualization, Exploratory Data Analysis, and Analytical Storytelling

## Track
Foundational

## Credit Hours
3

## Core Purpose

Cultivate visual analytical judgment: the ability to inspect data carefully, detect structure and anomalies, communicate evidence honestly, show uncertainty, diagnose models visually, and tell analytical stories without distortion.

## Governing Principles

1. Data must be seen before it is modeled.
2. A chart is a claim.
3. Comparison is the heart of visualization.
4. Scale shapes perception.
5. Aggregates can mislead.
6. Uncertainty should be visible.
7. Storytelling must not outrun evidence.
8. Visualization is reasoning, not decoration.

## Core Questions

- What does this dataset contain?
- What is missing, strange, skewed, or suspicious?
- What comparison matters?
- What chart type fits the question?
- What uncertainty should be shown?
- What does the chart reveal?
- What does the chart hide?
- What would make the visualization misleading?
- What does the audience need to understand?

## Lesson 1

### Title
Data Must Be Seen Before It Is Modeled

### Central Problem
Exploratory data analysis is not a preliminary chore. It is the first serious conversation with the data. Before modeling, the analyst must inspect distributions, missingness, outliers, categories, units, time ranges, and the meaning of each row.

### Required Concepts

- exploratory data analysis
- distribution
- missingness
- outlier
- unit
- variable type
- row-level meaning
- data quality
- visualization
- uncertainty

### Required Outputs

- EDA checklist
- initial inspection notebook
- oral-defense answer explaining why modeling before inspection is dangerous
