# Module 13: AI Ethics, Governance, Fairness, and Responsible Systems

## Track
Methods & Systems

## Credit Hours
5

## Core Purpose

Cultivate responsible sociotechnical judgment: the ability to evaluate, design, and govern AI systems according to technical performance, stakeholder harm, fairness, privacy, accountability, institutional context, and deployment risk.

## Governing Principles

1. AI systems are sociotechnical systems.
2. Fairness is contextual, contested, and mathematically constrained.
3. Bias begins before modeling.
4. Privacy is contextual and institutional.
5. Transparency is insufficient without contestability.
6. Accountability requires governance structures.
7. Technical fixes cannot solve all social problems.
8. Deployment is an ethical and institutional decision.

## Core Questions

- Who is affected by this system?
- What decision does it influence?
- What data produced the model behavior?
- What historical or institutional biases may be encoded?
- What fairness definition is relevant?
- What errors harm whom?
- What privacy risks exist?
- Who can contest the system?
- Who is accountable when it fails?
- Should the system be deployed?

## Lesson 1

### Title
AI Systems Are Sociotechnical Systems

### Central Problem
An AI system is not just a model. It includes data, labels, objectives, interfaces, users, institutions, policies, incentives, feedback loops, and affected communities. Ethical evaluation must therefore analyze the whole system rather than only model performance.

### Required Concepts

- sociotechnical system
- stakeholder
- harm model
- deployment context
- data-generating process
- institutional workflow
- accountability
- contestability
- governance

### Required Outputs

- stakeholder and system-boundary map
- short memo explaining why model accuracy is insufficient
- oral-defense answer defining who is accountable for system failure
