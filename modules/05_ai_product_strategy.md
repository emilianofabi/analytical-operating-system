# Module 05: AI Product Strategy, Systems Design, and Technological Innovation

## Track
Integration & Judgment

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate AI product judgment: the ability to determine when AI is useful, what problem it should solve, what system architecture is required, how success should be measured, and whether the product creates durable value under real constraints.

## Governing Principles

1. Technology is not product.
2. AI should solve a real problem, not decorate a workflow.
3. Product value depends on users, context, reliability, cost, and adoption.
4. Model capability is not system reliability.
5. Data strategy is product strategy.
6. Evaluation must reflect the task and user context.
7. Product metrics are proxies for value.
8. AI products require governance, monitoring, and feedback loops.

## Core Questions

- What user problem is being solved?
- Why is AI necessary?
- What would a non-AI solution look like?
- What data is required?
- What quality threshold must the system meet?
- What errors matter?
- How will success be measured?
- What workflow will the product enter?
- What human oversight is required?
- What creates defensibility?
- What risks emerge at scale?

## Lesson 1

### Title
When Should a Product Use AI?

### Central Problem
AI should not be treated as a default product ingredient. The first product question is whether the user problem requires prediction, generation, retrieval, classification, ranking, automation, or reasoning under uncertainty, and whether AI improves the workflow enough to justify cost, risk, and complexity.

### Required Concepts

- user problem
- workflow
- AI suitability
- non-AI baseline
- evaluation threshold
- error cost
- adoption friction
- product value
- deployment risk

### Required Outputs

- AI suitability memo
- comparison table of AI and non-AI approaches
- oral-defense answer explaining why model capability alone does not justify product adoption
