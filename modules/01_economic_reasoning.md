# Module 01: Economic Reasoning, Incentives, and Systems Analysis

## Track
Foundational

## Credit Hours
4

## Core Purpose

Cultivate economic judgment: the ability to reason about scarcity, incentives, constraints, tradeoffs, opportunity cost, equilibrium, information, institutions, and strategic behavior.

## Governing Principles

1. Scarcity makes choice unavoidable.
2. Every decision has an opportunity cost.
3. Marginal thinking clarifies tradeoffs.
4. Incentives shape behavior but do not mechanically determine it.
5. Markets are institutional arrangements, not natural abstractions.
6. Prices communicate information but also reflect power, rules, and constraints.
7. Strategic behavior requires reasoning about other agents' responses.
8. Economic models clarify mechanisms through controlled omission.

## Core Questions

- What is scarce?
- Who is choosing?
- What alternatives are available?
- What incentives shape the decision?
- What constraints bind the agents?
- What tradeoffs cannot be avoided?
- What information is missing or asymmetric?
- What equilibrium or system-level pattern might emerge?
- Who gains, who loses, and under what institutional rules?

## Lesson 1

### Title
Opportunity Cost and the Logic of Scarcity

### Central Problem
Economic reasoning begins from scarcity. Because resources, time, attention, capital, labor, and institutional capacity are limited, every choice excludes alternatives. Opportunity cost is the value of the best forgone alternative and is the foundation for disciplined reasoning about tradeoffs.

### Required Concepts

- scarcity
- choice
- opportunity cost
- explicit cost
- implicit cost
- sunk cost
- tradeoff
- constraint
- marginal comparison

### Required Outputs

- short memo identifying the opportunity costs in a technical or educational decision
- table comparing explicit costs, implicit costs, and forgone alternatives
- oral-defense answer explaining why opportunity cost is not the same as monetary cost
