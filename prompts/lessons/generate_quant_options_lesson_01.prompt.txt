Lesson title:
Alpha Is Not Observed Return, and Options Are Not Just Directional Bets

Purpose:
Teach quantitative finance and options trading through the AOS methodology. The lesson should help a student studying economics and data science with a statistics minor understand both school-relevant concepts and practical trading research discipline.

Student background:
Assume the learner knows introductory finance and basic options terminology, but wants crisp refreshers when useful. Use real math, but always translate formulas into plain English. Be rigorous without becoming needlessly obscure.

AOS methodology:
Use these principles throughout:
- Purpose before technique.
- Identification before estimation.
- Models are controlled omissions.
- Data is produced, not found.
- Metrics are proxies, not values.
- Prediction is not explanation.
- Infrastructure is epistemic.
- Markets are adaptive systems.
- Risk management is governance.
- Synthesis requires visible artifacts.

Core thesis:
Observed trading performance is not automatically alpha. It may reflect risk exposure, noise, selection bias, overfitting, transaction costs, slippage, market impact, regime luck, volatility risk premia, liquidity effects, or market adaptation.

Options thesis:
Options are not just leveraged directional bets. They are instruments for trading direction, volatility, convexity, time decay, skew, tail risk, liquidity, path dependency, and probability distributions.

Core conceptual tension:
A beginner sees profit and thinks "edge." A serious quant asks: was this true alpha, compensated risk, volatility exposure, lucky timing, cheap leverage, hidden tail risk, or a backtest illusion?

Core formulas to include and explain:

CAPM:
R_i - r_f = alpha_i + beta_i(R_m - r_f) + epsilon_i

Multifactor model:
R_i - r_f = alpha_i + beta_1 F_1 + beta_2 F_2 + ... + beta_k F_k + epsilon_i

Sharpe ratio:
Sharpe = E[R_p - r_f] / sigma(R_p - r_f)

t-statistic:
t = mean(R) / (sigma(R) / sqrt(T))

Portfolio return:
R_p,t+1 = w_t' r_t+1

Portfolio variance:
Var(R_p) = w' Sigma w

Net return:
R_net = R_gross - transaction_costs - slippage - market_impact

Put-call parity:
C - P = S - K e^(-rT)

Black-Scholes call price:
C = S N(d1) - K e^(-rT) N(d2)

Delta:
Delta = partial V / partial S

Gamma:
Gamma = partial^2 V / partial S^2

Theta:
Theta = partial V / partial t

Vega:
Vega = partial V / partial sigma

Options analysis best practices to introduce:
- start with thesis before choosing a structure;
- separate direction, volatility, time, and event risk;
- compare implied volatility to realized volatility;
- check liquidity and bid-ask spread;
- define max loss before entry;
- use scenario analysis;
- understand Greek exposure;
- avoid strategies where costs dominate edge;
- paper trade before live deployment;
- journal thesis, entry, exit, and post-trade review.
